CborLens

CBOR is everywhere in security tokens, COSE signatures and IoT payloads - and the same data can encode a dozen ways. See exactly what's in the blob, and whether it's canonical enough to hash.

Open CborLens

Canonicality verdict

Non-shortest integers, indefinite lengths, unsorted map keys - the three things that break deterministic encoding, flagged item by item.

Full RFC 8949

Every major type: 64-bit ints (BigInt-exact), byte and text strings, arrays, maps, tags, and half/single/double floats.

Duplicate keys caught

Maps with the same key twice are invalid CBOR but decode silently in most tools. Not here.

Nothing uploads

The decoder runs in your browser against local bytes. Your payloads never leave your machine.

What it reads

  • Unsigned and negative integers up to 64 bits, exact via BigInt (no float rounding above 2^53)
  • Definite and indefinite byte/text strings, with chunk counts and UTF-8 validation
  • Arrays and maps, nested, with duplicate-key detection and canonical key-order checking (length-first, then bytewise)
  • Semantic tags - datetimes, epochs, bignums - shown with their inner value
  • Simple values and all three float widths, including manual float16 decoding
  • Truncation and malformed-input errors instead of silent mis-decodes

Checked by an independent decoder

The shared engine (engine.js) runs in Node against a corpus decoded by a completely separate Python CBOR implementation (tests/build_corpus.py): two independent decoders must agree on every value, every item count, every depth, and every canonicality violation. 74 checks pass (node tests/run_tests.js).
